# Phones: leave the apps, keep the sites

Native apps are where the pull toward endless scrolling is strongest, and no
blocker can reach inside them: an app ships its own feed, its own player and its
own "up next", and decides what you see. On the web, your blocker gets the last
word. So the setup on a phone is:

1. **Uninstall the native apps** (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, X, Reddit,
   Facebook, LinkedIn…). With the apps gone, links people send you open in your
   browser, where the lists apply.
2. **Install a browser + blocker** (the [website](https://aymericbeaumet.github.io/intentional/#install)
   has the steps per phone):
   - **Android:** Firefox + uBlock Origin is the full experience, redirects included.
   - **iPhone/iPad:** Safari + uBlock Origin Lite (free), plus the free
     Userscripts extension for redirects. AdGuard for iOS works too; redirects
     need its Premium "Advanced protection".
3. **Put the intentional views on your home screen.** Each site lists its
   intentional views (YouTube → Subscriptions, Instagram → Following, Twitch →
   Following…). Open one in your browser, then Share → Add to Home Screen.
4. **Make the shortcut open in your browser.**
   - **iOS 26 and later:** "Add to Home Screen" shows an _Open as Web App_
     toggle, on by default. Turn it **off**. The icon then opens a normal Safari
     tab, where your blocker runs. Nothing official says Safari extensions run
     inside home-screen web apps, so don't count on it.
   - **Android:** add the shortcut from Firefox's menu, so it opens in Firefox.
     A web app installed from Chrome opens in Chrome, which has no extensions.
5. **Optional hardening:** turn off the sites' push notifications; use Screen
   Time (iOS) or Digital Wellbeing (Android) so reinstalling an app takes a
   deliberate step.

## What you keep

Everything you'd go to an app for on purpose: a video someone sent you, the
channels and people you follow, search, messages and notifications. What
disappears is what was chosen for you.
